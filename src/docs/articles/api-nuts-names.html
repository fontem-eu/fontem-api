Region names in 24 languages (public API)

Eurostat names each NUTS region twice: in the national language (Αττική) and transliterated into Latin (Attiki). Nobody publishes all 24 official languages. We assemble them, so /nuts answers both "what is EL3 called in French" and "which region is Lisbonne".

Endpoints

  • GET /api/nuts — vintage, build date, languages, coverage per language and per level, sources and licence. Read this first.
  • GET /api/nuts/regions?level=&country=&limit=&offset= — every region with all its known names, ordered by code.
  • GET /api/nuts/regions/{code} — one region, its ancestor chain and its direct children.
  • GET /api/nuts/search?q=&lang=&level=&country= — a name in any of the 24 languages, or a code, to regions. Each hit reports which form matched and in which language.
  • GET /api/nuts/gazetteer.json — the whole artifact, for keeping a local copy.
  • GET /api/nuts/regions.csv — one row per region per name: code,level,country,parent,language,name,kind.

What a record holds

code, level, country, parent, name_native and name_latn (the two Eurostat forms), names (a name per language, only for the languages one is known in), aliases, and sources — which of Eurostat, the EU Publications Office or Wikidata that record's names came from.

Coverage is uneven, and says so

About 86% of the 1,798 regions carry at least one translation, averaging roughly 16 of the 24 languages; it is thinnest at NUTS 3 and in Maltese. A region with no translation falls back to its transliteration, which is what makes it findable at all. Machine translation is not used: it invents plausible place names, and a wrong name is worse than an untranslated one.

Search notes

Matching ignores case and accents, and treats Greek final sigma and German ß the way the index does, so αττικη finds Αττική and bergstrasse finds Bergstraße. Names of regions, not of settlements: a NUTS 3 unit is findable by the metro region Eurostat gives it (Athina), but there is no city gazetteer behind this.
